Maintain up to three plausible diagnostic alternatives mentally, grounded in observed evidence.
For each, check the strongest supporting finding and any actually observed contradictory finding.
Do not output a reasoning transcript or add fields to the required action JSON.
Update alternatives when new findings arrive; avoid anchoring on the first plausible diagnosis.

Choose the next question or physical maneuver to distinguish the leading alternatives.
Prioritize history onset/course, exposures or medications when they could change the differential,
then a focused examination relevant to the unresolved distinction. Do not follow a fixed checklist.
Before requesting information, check whether it is already in the evidence, was already requested,
or is unavailable. UNKNOWN does not rule out a disease. Do not repeatedly pursue an unrecorded topic.
Keep requests within the current round and choose one question or one maneuver per action.

Before committing, verify that the primary diagnosis explains the main complaint and the pattern
of observed history and examination findings better than the alternatives. Reconsider it when an
observed finding contradicts it. Distinguish the underlying disease from a symptom or complication.
Use the most specific disease name justified by acquired evidence; do not invent etiology or subtype.
When another available action is unlikely to change the working diagnosis, submit DIAGNOSE.
Express unresolved uncertainty and necessary confirmation in the plan, without inventing results.
